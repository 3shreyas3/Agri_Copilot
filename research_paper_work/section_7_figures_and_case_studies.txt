SECTION VII: RESULTS, PERFORMANCE EVALUATION, AND CASE STUDIES

This section presents the empirical validation, runtime performance benchmarks, and real-world case studies obtained from the execution of the AgriCopilot platform. The figures illustrate the live client-side behavior of the system across pedological classification, contradiction detection, biophysical crop stress mitigation, post-harvest profit multipliers, land-segmented institutional finance, and interstate logistics market redirection.


Figure 1: Client-Side Pedological Vision Inference HUD Showing 9D Colorimetric Feature Extraction and Continuous Softmax Class Probabilities.

Description and Architectural Analysis:
Figure 1 illustrates the in-browser pedological computer vision HUD executing within Section 3 of the Farmer Input Form. Upon the ingestion of an optical soil photograph (or calibrated benchmark sample), the client-side analyzer processes pixel arrays directly in browser RAM via HTML5 Canvas memory buffers without invoking remote cloud inference. 

The image preview card displays the uploaded sample accompanied by an extracted colorimetric swatch, displaying real-world RGB channel averages and the linearized perceptual lightness value (CIE-L*). Adjacent to the preview, the Machine Learning Pedological Classification card renders the top-1 predicted soil order along with a quantitative certainty badge. The card highlights the runner-up classification and presents continuous probability progress bars derived from the temperature-scaled Softmax activation function (T = 1.3). As evidenced in the visual readout, the model achieves sharp class separation, allocating dominant probability to the ground-truth soil order (e.g., Red and Yellow Soil - Alfisols at 84% certainty, with secondary probability distributed to morphologically adjacent orders such as Laterite). This client-side execution benchmark operates deterministically in under 50 milliseconds on standard consumer hardware, validating that complex pedological classification can be performed locally with zero cloud API overhead.


Figure 2: Automated Pedological Contradiction Detection Flagging Visual Soil Photo Discrepancies Against Farmer-Selected Soil Orders.

Description and Architectural Analysis:
Figure 2 demonstrates the automated contradiction detection mechanism designed to safeguard farmers against inappropriate agronomic planning caused by erroneous manual soil entries. In this experimental scenario, the farmer manually selected Black Soil (Vertisols) from the input dropdown, but uploaded an optical photograph corresponding to Red Soil (Alfisols).

Rather than accepting contradictory inputs or executing silent misclassifications, AgriCopilot’s verification engine detects the divergence between the user-declared class and the vision model’s mathematical centroid prediction. The system generates an immediate high-visibility amber warning banner: Soil Type Mismatch Warning: The uploaded photo looks like Red Soil (Alfisols), but you selected Black Soil. Accompanying this diagnostic alert, the interface provides a 1-Click Update to Detected Soil corrective action button. Activating this control immediately aligns the form state with physical ground truth, automatically recalculating downstream crop suitability envelopes and preventing the farmer from applying inappropriate fertilizers or water schedules. In the underlying advisory engine, an unresolved mismatch incurs a specific -1.6 point biophysical penalty, verifying the system's sensitivity to visual-versus-stated edaphic discrepancies.


Figure 3: Biophysical Stress Penalty Alert Paired with Pareto-Optimal Alternate Crop Recommendation and 1-Click Adaptive State Switching.

Description and Architectural Analysis:
Figure 3 captures the agronomic stress evaluation and dynamic mitigation engine responding to acute environmental hazards. In this case study, a farmer in Davanagere district attempted to cultivate Paddy (Rice) under adverse field parameters: Red Soil (Alfisols), an ambient temperature of 38 degrees Celsius, and seasonal rainfall of 450 millimeters.

Because Paddy is a water-intensive crop requiring saturated clay soils and moderate temperatures, the biophysical engine evaluates extreme departures from ICAR cardinal limits. The system triggers a prominent High Agronomic Risk alert detailing itemized biological stresses: severe thermal stress (38 degrees Celsius exceeding the 32 degrees Celsius maximum threshold, threatening pollen sterility and flower abortion), acute hydrological drought deficit (450 mm versus the 1000 mm minimum requirement), and structural soil incompatibility. Crucially, the engine does not merely output a failure warning. It queries the regional crop database using multi-factor suitability scoring and surfaces Jowar (Sorghum) as the highest-ranked alternative, highlighting a 98% agro-climatic fit, drought resilience (requiring only 450 mm rainfall), and statutory Minimum Support Price (MSP) economic assurance. The interface features a prominent 1-Click Switch to Jowar button, which atomically substitutes the crop parameter and triggers an instantaneous re-evaluation of the farm plan.


Figure 4: Plant Health Score Recovery from High Risk (Below 40%) to Optimal Vitality (Above 90%) Following Adaptive Alternate Crop Mitigation.

Description and Architectural Analysis:
Figure 4 presents a comparative before-and-after evaluation demonstrating the quantitative recovery of the composite Plant Health Score within the Advisory Report dashboard. 

In the initial high-stress baseline scenario (Paddy under drought conditions), the circular SVG health gauge visualizes a critical score of 38% (High Risk Level), with the interface highlighting accumulated deductions for thermal excess, moisture deficit, and soil order mismatch. Following the activation of the 1-Click Alternate Crop Switch to Jowar, the advisory engine recalculates all biological parameters against Jowar’s physiological thresholds. In the post-mitigation state, the Plant Health Score surges to 94% (Healthy Match), the risk level drops to Low, all environmental stress tags turn green, and the advisory report issues a positive agronomic forecast. This empirical case verifies that AgriCopilot transforms passive risk warnings into an active, closed-loop decision support mechanism that guides smallholders from catastrophic crop vulnerability to climate-resilient productivity.


Figure 5: Interactive Value-Addition Profit Multiplier Comparing Raw Distress Wholesale Realization Against Primary On-Farm Grading.

Description and Architectural Analysis:
Figure 5 illustrates the Value-Addition Profit Multiplier Simulator situated within the Governance and Finance module. The component directly addresses the post-harvest margin loss incurred when farmers sell uncleaned, ungraded field-run produce directly into wholesale mandi auctions.

The interactive interface features a real-time yield slider (calibrated in quintals) tied to the farmer’s operational landholding. Utilizing the crop profile’s statutory MSP and projected market price band, the simulator computes a side-by-side financial comparison between Raw Distress Auction Revenue and Value-Added Processed Revenue. In the showcased Tomato harvesting scenario, raw unclassified auction sales at depressed glut prices yield a baseline realization. By applying simple primary interventions—such as mechanical grading into Grade-A and Grade-B crates, surface washing, and solar drying—the simulator models an empirical net revenue expansion of +28% to +35%, translating to an immediate net profit gain of over 34,000 INR on a standard smallholder harvest lot. This provides transparent, interactive visual proof that post-harvest primary processing significantly buffers growers against local mandi price collapses.


Figure 6: Automated Landholding Segmentation Directing Smallholders (Less Than 2.0 Acres) to 4% KCC Micro-Loans and Commercial Growers (2.0 Acres or More) to AIF Infrastructure Credit.

Description and Architectural Analysis:
Figure 6 demonstrates the algorithmic credit triage engine, which partitions banking guidance based on statutory landholding thresholds to eliminate documentation barriers and informal debt traps.

The interface presents tailored financial cards dynamically filtered by the farmer's declared acreage:
- Smallholder Scenario (Landholding less than 2.0 acres): The portal automatically prioritizes the subsidized Kisan Credit Card (KCC) scheme, displaying the effective 4.0% per annum borrowing rate (reflecting the 3.0% prompt repayment incentive subvention), the 1.6 Lakh INR collateral-free borrowing ceiling, Joint Liability Group (JLG) peer-guaranteed credit, and direct integration with PM-KISAN.
- Commercial Cultivator Scenario (Landholding 2.0 acres or more): The engine dynamically reconfigures to surface large-scale capital development programs, highlighting the Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (AIF) with its 3.0% interest subvention on term loans up to 2.0 Crore INR, alongside Electronic Negotiable Warehouse Receipt (e-NWR) pledge financing up to 75% produce valuation.
Each card embeds explicit DecisionReason justifications and verified official Government of India web endpoints, replacing arbitrary credit rejections with transparent, accessible institutional pathways.


Figure 7: Geospatial Interstate Market Redirection Map Routing Produce Along National Highway Corridors from Glutted Local Mandis to Deficit Terminal Hubs.

Description and Architectural Analysis:
Figure 7 showcases the geospatial logistics arbitrage engine operating within Tab 3 of the application. The system visualizes an interactive Leaflet GIS map with polyline highway corridors connecting origin farming mandis to high-demand interstate terminal consumption hubs.

In this case study, a local oversupply glut is simulated in Davanagere district, where a 85% regional sowing popularity triggers a wholesale price crash down to 1,200 INR per quintal at the local APMC mandi. The geospatial solver evaluates national highway corridors (NH 48) and identifies Mumbai Vashi APMC as a high-demand deficit market trading at 2,450 INR per quintal. 
The interface computes the road haulage distance (785 km, reflecting a 1.28 highway tortuosity factor) and models commercial freight haulage costs:
Freight = (785 / 100) * 36 + 22 = 305 INR per Quintal
Deducting freight from the gross spread of 1,250 INR per quintal yields an attractive Net Arbitrage Margin of 945 INR per quintal. For a standard 50-quintal commercial truckload, the platform projects an extra net profit of +47,250 INR (a 78% return on investment over local distressed auction prices) with a transit duration of approximately 18.7 hours. This geospatial visualization provides actionable market intelligence enabling smallholder cooperatives and Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs) to bypass localized market failures.
