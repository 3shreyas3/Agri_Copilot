SECTION III: SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE AND DESIGN OVERVIEW

To bridge the structural divide between scientific agronomy, spatial market logistics, and institutional financial welfare, AgriCopilot is engineered as a decoupled, layered, edge-native decision intelligence system. Unlike conventional centralized agri-portals that rely on heavy cloud backends and continuous server polling, AgriCopilot executes its core predictive models, agrometeorological rules engines, and geospatial routing algorithms directly within the user client environment. This section delineates the multi-tiered system architecture, the edge execution paradigm, and the authoritative agronomic and economic knowledge bases integrated into the platform.


A. End-to-End System Architecture (Figure 1)

The AgriCopilot platform operates across a hierarchical six-layer architecture designed for deterministic execution, low latency, and modular extensibility. The information pipeline coordinates user sensory ingestion, client-side inference, constraint-satisfaction scoring, and explainable multi-modal presentation:

Layer 1: Farmer Profile and Agronomic Input Layer
The system ingests a structured dossier directly from the farmer or village extension agent:
- Demographic Profile: Farmer Name, Operational Landholding Size (in Acres), Administrative District (e.g., Davanagere, Chitradurga, Belagavi, or Kolar), and Village Coordinates.
- Agro-Climatic Parameters: Sown Crop Selection, Farmer-Stated Soil Order, Ambient Temperature in degrees Celsius, Seasonal Rainfall in millimeters.
- Sensory Soil Artifact: Raw optical photograph captured via smartphone camera or uploaded from local storage, or calibrated benchmark sample selection.
- Sowing Market Dynamics: Regional Sowing Popularity Percentage, indicating the proportion of local district farmers cultivating the identical crop.

Layer 2: In-Browser Computer Vision and Sensory Pre-processing Layer
Implemented via native HTML5 Canvas memory buffers within trainedSoilModel.js and soilImageAnalyzer.js:
- Employs an adaptive 5-zone spatial pooling mask (65% center, 35% outer) with luminance outlier filtration (Lum between 10 and 245) to remove fingers, lens vignetting, and specular glare.
- Converts linearized sRGB to device-independent CIE-Lab color space under standard D65 illuminants.
- Extracts a 9-dimensional pedological vector (CIE-L, CIE-a, CIE-b, Hematite Redness Index, Soil Organic Carbon Index, Spatial Laplacian Roughness, and Gray-World normalized chromaticities).
- Executes continuous Softmax classification (T = 1.3) across six ICAR-NBSS&LUP soil orders and detects visual-versus-stated soil contradictions.

Layer 3: Core Algorithmic and Decision Intelligence Engines
Encapsulated within advisoryEngine.js and marketRedirectionData.js:
- Biophysical Crop Health and Stress Engine: Evaluates non-linear deviations against physiological thermal limits and moisture water balances, applying structural soil and visual mismatch penalties.
- Resilient Alternate Crop Engine: Computes a multi-factor suitability index (40% soil, 35% rainfall, 25% temperature) across the candidate crop database to surface optimal substitutes during high-risk conditions.
- Sowing Saturation Supply-Elasticity Engine: Evaluates regional planting popularity, applying economic multipliers (0.78 to 0.85 for glut regimes; 1.15 to 1.28 for scarcity regimes) to forecast wholesale prices.
- Interstate Mandi Arbitrage GIS Solver: Calculates road transport distances using Haversine formulations adjusted by a 1.28 highway tortuosity factor, modeling freight deductions per quintal to identify net profitable destination mandis.
- Two-Tier Institutional Credit Triage Engine: Dynamically partitions banking guidance based on landholding (Smallholders less than 2.0 acres versus Commercial Cultivators 2.0 acres or more).

Layer 4: Authoritative Ground-Truth Knowledge Bases
Integrates scientific agronomic thresholds from ICAR, soil orders from ICAR-NBSS&LUP, wholesale APMC benchmarks from Agmarknet, MSP standards from CACP, and climatological normals from the India Meteorological Department (IMD).

Layer 5: Dual-Attribute Explainability (XAI) Layer
Binds every diagnostic score, loan qualification, and crop rejection to transparent DecisionReason and actionable Solution text blocks, ensuring absolute algorithmic accountability.

Layer 6: Interactive 4-Tab User Interface and Multilingual Speech HUD
Rendered via React 18, Tailwind CSS, and Leaflet GIS across four dedicated workspace modules:
- Tab 1: Farmer Input Dossier and Soil Diagnostics HUD.
- Tab 2: Comprehensive Advisory Report and 1-Click Alternate Crop Switch.
- Tab 3: Interstate Market Redirection Leaflet Map and Highway Corridor Visualizer.
- Tab 4: Governance Policies, Institutional Finance Portal, and Value-Addition Calculator.
This layer is complemented by a persistent Marquee Mandi Ticker Strip and native HTML5 Web Speech API synthesis across six Indian languages.


B. The Client-Side Edge Computing Paradigm

A foundational architectural contribution of AgriCopilot is its complete reliance on a Client-Side Edge Computing paradigm, realized as an offline-capable Single-Page Application (SPA) built on React 18.3.1 and Vite 6.0.7. Traditional precision agriculture platforms depend on a client-server architecture where raw images and agronomic queries are transmitted over cellular networks to cloud-hosted backend servers running Python, Django, or Flask. In rural agrarian contexts, this centralized model exhibits three severe operational failures:

1. Latency Bottlenecks and Telemetry Timeouts: Transmitting uncompressed 5MB to 12MB smartphone camera photographs across rural 2G/3G networks introduces severe upload delays (15 to 45 seconds) and frequent socket dropouts. By performing pixel manipulation directly in browser RAM via the HTML5 Canvas getImageData interface, AgriCopilot executes complete 9D pedological vector extraction and Softmax classification in under 50 milliseconds directly on client hardware.
2. Zero Cloud Infrastructure Cost: By executing all inference engines (pedological computer vision, agrometeorological stress models, Haversine routing, and credit triage) locally on the client runtime, AgriCopilot eliminates cloud GPU and server hosting expenses. This makes the platform infinitely scalable for state agriculture departments and rural farmer cooperatives at zero marginal operational compute cost.
3. Strict Data Privacy and Offline Resilience: Handheld agronomic data, landholding boundaries, and geotagged farm coordinates remain strictly within the client browser sandbox and localStorage. Once the lightweight production bundle (under 600 KB minified) is loaded or cached via progressive web techniques, the entire diagnostic pipeline executes seamlessly even when cellular connectivity is completely unavailable in remote agricultural fields.


C. Authoritative Agronomic and Economic Knowledge Bases

To guarantee scientific validity and eliminate hallucinated advice, AgriCopilot does not rely on unconstrained generative language models for numerical evaluation. Instead, its core decision intelligence engines operate deterministically on validated agronomic, pedological, and economic knowledge repositories curated from premier Indian agricultural institutions:

1. ICAR and State Agricultural Universities (SAUs) Agronomic Envelopes:
The platform embeds comprehensive biological profiles for 12 major Indian field, pulse, and commercial crops (Paddy, Maize, Ragi, Jowar, Cotton, Groundnut, Sugarcane, Tomato, Onion, Soybean, Wheat, and Bengal Gram). For each crop, the database defines strict physiological envelopes:
- Thermal Bounds: Absolute minimum (T_min), absolute maximum (T_max), and optimal physiological temperature (T_opt) governing vegetative development, anthesis, and grain filling.
- Hydrological Bounds: Minimum seasonal rainfall (R_min), maximum tolerance (R_max), and optimal water requirement (R_opt) in millimeters.
- Pedological Affinity: Explicit lists of ideal soil orders and compatible soil families, with non-linear penalties for incompatible physical textures.
- Regional Pest Epidemiology: Localized pest vulnerabilities mapped by administrative district (e.g., Fall Armyworm Spodoptera frugiperda in Davanagere Maize, Helicoverpa armigera in Cotton, and Tuta absoluta in Tomato) paired with natural biological countermeasures.

2. ICAR-NBSS&LUP Pedological Soil Order Classification:
Calibrated directly against the taxonomic standards of the National Bureau of Soil Survey and Land Use Planning (ICAR-NBSS&LUP), the vision model establishes reference centroid vectors and tolerance thresholds for the six dominant Indian soil orders:
- Vertisols (Black Regur): High montmorillonite clay, high organic carbon index (0.76), deep shrink-swell clods, and low lightness (L = 24.0).
- Alfisols (Red and Yellow Soils): Porous friable loams characterized by high ferric hematite redness (RI = 0.29) and elevated a* chromaticity (a = 36.0).
- Oxisols and Ultisols (Laterite Soils): Leached vesicular structures rich in iron and aluminum sesquioxides with dark rusty hues (L = 30.0, a = 30.0, b = 25.0).
- Arenosols (Sandy Loam): Highly porous, coarse quartz sands with elevated b* values (b = 26.0) and high lightness (L = 58.0).
- Heavy Clayey Soils: Dense, compact silt-clays with low drainage and moderate redness.
- Inceptisols and Entisols (Alluvial Loam): Highly fertile riverine deposits with balanced organic matter, moderate lightness (L = 50.0), and fine spatial Laplacian texture.

3. Agmarknet and APMC Wholesale Market Intelligence:
The economic engine integrates wholesale price benchmarks and baseline modal price ranges derived from Agmarknet (the Agricultural Marketing Information Network under the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare) across major regional APMC mandis.

4. CACP Minimum Support Price (MSP) and FRP Benchmarks:
Official price assurance baselines are established using statutory Minimum Support Prices (MSP) recommended by the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP) for the 2024-2026 kharif and rabi seasons, ensuring that price forecasting algorithms remain anchored to sovereign floor guarantees.

5. IMD Climatological Normals:
Agrometeorological temperature and precipitation benchmarks are aligned with the 30-year climatological normal datasets published by the India Meteorological Department (IMD) for southern and central agro-ecological zones.

6. Central and State Institutional Welfare Schemes:
The governance hub integrates regulatory eligibility rules, subsidy slabs, and direct digital portal endpoints for core national and state welfare initiatives:
- PM-KISAN: Direct Benefit Transfer offering 6,000 INR annual income support.
- PM Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY): Subsidized multi-hazard crop insurance (2% premium for Kharif, 1.5% for Rabi).
- Kisan Credit Card (KCC): Subsidized 4.0% revolving credit up to 1.6 Lakh INR collateral-free.
- Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (AIF): 3.0% interest subvention on term loans up to 2 Crore INR for post-harvest infrastructure.
- Electronic Negotiable Warehouse Receipts (e-NWR): Pledge financing up to 75% produce valuation under WDRA accredited warehouses.
- State-Specific Portals: Karnataka FRUITS (Farmer Registration and Unified Beneficiary Information System) and Raitha Samparka Kendra extension services.
